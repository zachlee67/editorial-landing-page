# Industry Section Map

The house style (see `SKILL.md`) has a fixed structural skeleton: hero → trust strip →
"is this you" cards → mechanism explainer → benefits grid → offer details → \[secondary
offer, if any\] → comparison table (if two offers) → proof (before/after or case studies) →
credibility → testimonials → FAQ → final CTA → form → footer.

What changes per industry is what each generic slot actually *means*, and which slots get
dropped because they don't apply. This file is a starting map, not a rulebook — read it, then
still ask the operator rather than assuming. If a request lands in an industry not listed here,
do the same kind of quick research (search for "[industry] landing page best practices") before
guessing at the mapping.

Sourced from a 2026 review of conversion guidance for SaaS, real estate, home services,
restaurants/F&B, and professional services landing pages (see Sources at the bottom).

## Aesthetic clinic / medical aesthetics (the original vertical)

This is the skeleton's origin case — see `aesthetic-clinic-landing-page` for the fully worked
version. Symptom cards = literal symptoms, before/after = literal photos, mechanism = how the
device/technique works, comparison table = primary vs secondary treatment.

## Home services (renovation, aircon servicing, pest control, plumbing, contractors)

- **"Is this you" cards** → literal signs the service is needed (e.g. "aircon not cooling
  properly", "water stains on the ceiling"). This maps almost one-to-one to the clinic version.
- **Mechanism explainer** → how the specific method/technology works (e.g. what makes their
  chemical wash different from a basic service).
- **Before/after** → literal before/after photos. Real project photos outperform stock images
  here more than in almost any other vertical — flag as a launch blocker if the operator has
  none, don't substitute stock photos of someone else's job.
- **Trust strip** → licensed/certified badges, years in business, response-time guarantee
  (e.g. "same-day service"), not FDA-style claims.
- **Comparison table** → service tiers (one-time visit vs maintenance contract) if the operator
  sells both.
- **Contact CTA** → WhatsApp is standard and expected in Malaysia/SG home services; keep the
  sticky float.

## Real estate (agent lead-gen, project launch, renovation-focused agents)

- **"Is this you" cards** → buyer-persona cards (first-time buyer, upgrader, investor) or
  unit-type cards, not symptom framing.
- **Mechanism explainer** → replace with a "why this project/location" section: developer track
  record, location advantages, connectivity — the thing that makes this listing different from
  the next one on the same street.
- **Before/after** → showflat/interior gallery, or literal before/after for renovation-focused
  agents.
- **Trust strip** → developer license/REN number, awards, units sold/remaining.
- **Comparison table** → unit-type comparison (studio vs 2BR vs 3BR pricing/size), not two
  unrelated offers.
- **Contact CTA** → WhatsApp/call agent, book a showflat viewing slot.
- Note: industry median conversion is notably lower here (2.7-3.6%) than other verticals per
  2026 benchmarks — don't over-promise conversion projections to the operator for this vertical.

## Education (tuition centres, courses, bootcamps)

- **"Is this you" cards** → the pain points a struggling student/parent recognizes (e.g. "marks
  dropping despite tuition", "loses focus after 20 minutes").
- **Mechanism explainer** → the teaching methodology or curriculum structure.
- **Before/after** → real student results (exam score improvements, grade jumps) — testimonials
  and score data only, never a fabricated "+40% improvement" stat.
- **Trust strip** → tutor credentials, pass rates, years teaching the syllabus.
- **Comparison table** → course-tier comparison (regular vs intensive/1-on-1), if applicable.
- **Contact CTA** → WhatsApp enquiry, book a trial class.

## Professional services (law, accounting, consulting, agencies)

- **"Is this you" cards** → the situations/problems that bring someone to this firm (e.g. "served
  with a legal notice", "missed three tax deadlines this year").
- **Mechanism explainer** → the engagement process (this already matches the "how it works"
  3-step pattern well: consult → scope → deliver).
- **Before/after** → case-study outcomes stated in real terms the operator can back up (e.g. "case
  resolved in 6 weeks"), not literal images.
- **Trust strip** → years of practice, cases handled, bar/board admission, notable clients (only
  if the operator can name them).
- **Comparison table** → retainer/package tiers, or "working with us vs going it alone."
- A fixed price or a clearly defined free first step (e.g. "free 15-minute consult") measurably
  reduces the anxiety that kills conversion in this vertical — push for one if the operator
  doesn't have one yet.
- **Contact CTA** → book a consultation call; WhatsApp works but a calendar booking link often
  converts better for higher-ticket B2B/professional engagements.

## F&B / restaurants / cafés

- This vertical breaks the skeleton the most. Food landing pages sell craving and atmosphere, not
  a mechanism.
- **"Is this you" cards** → usually drop this, or replace with occasion cards (date night, family
  gathering, corporate catering).
- **Mechanism explainer** → drop, or replace with a short "our story / ingredients" section if
  the operator has one worth telling.
- **Before/after** → drop. Replace with a real food/interior photo gallery. Real photos are
  mandatory here, more than any other vertical; do not ship this page with placeholder boxes
  where food photos should be. If none exist, that's a launch blocker, not a placeholder.
- **Trust strip** → review rating, halal/certification badges, delivery-partner logos.
- **Comparison table** → only if there are genuinely two offers (e.g. dine-in set menu vs private
  event package).
- **Contact CTA** → WhatsApp for reservations/catering enquiries, or a delivery-platform link for
  straight ordering.

## SaaS / software

- **Hero** → show the actual product interface (a real screenshot or short product clip), not an
  illustration. Product-forward heroes measurably outconvert concept art in 2026 SaaS benchmarks.
  Name the outcome in the headline, not the feature category.
- **"Is this you" cards** → pain-point cards (the workflow problems the software removes).
- **Mechanism explainer** → "how it works", same slot, same idea.
- **Trust strip** → integrations, security/compliance badges (SOC 2, uptime SLA), customer logos.
- **Before/after** → replace with product screenshots/demo GIF or a metrics-based case study
  ("cut reporting time from 3 days to 20 minutes"), never a fabricated stat.
- **Comparison table** → pricing tiers, or "us vs the old way / vs a named competitor" if the
  operator wants a direct comparison.
- **Contact CTA** → usually "start free trial" or "book a demo", not WhatsApp, unless the
  operator runs a WhatsApp-led sales motion (common for SEA SMB-focused SaaS).
- Recommended scroll order from 2026 research: hero (what do I get) → social proof (who else uses
  this) → how it works → pricing/commitment. Keep the form/CTA reachable earlier than this
  skeleton's "always at the very end" default if the operator's product has a low-friction signup
  — ask rather than assume.

## E-commerce / DTC product

- **"Is this you" cards** → the problem the product solves (works well for skincare, supplements,
  gadgets with a clear "before" state).
- **Mechanism explainer** → how the product/ingredient/tech works.
- **Before/after** → literal before/after, a strong fit for this vertical when the operator has
  real ones (skincare, fitness products) — otherwise drop rather than fake it.
- **Trust strip** → money-back guarantee, shipping info, verified review count.
- **Comparison table** → bundle/tier comparison (single unit vs 3-pack vs subscription).
- **Contact CTA** → direct "Add to Cart"/checkout is the norm; WhatsApp fits COD-style or
  informal SEA sellers, ask which applies.
